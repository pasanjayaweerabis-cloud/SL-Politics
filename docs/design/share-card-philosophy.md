# Quiet Masthead

A note before the philosophy: this is written for a public-record site, not a
gallery wall, and the brief for it is explicit that the usual 90%-visual,
subtle-reference instinct is tuned down here. What follows is a restrained
design system for one recurring object — a share card — not an art movement
with room for a hidden reference. The discipline is the same; the ambition is
smaller on purpose.

**Quiet Masthead** treats the share card the way a newspaper treats its own
nameplate: fixed, plain, and never competing with the story underneath it. A
masthead does not illustrate the news; it identifies the publication carrying
it, once, in the same place, every time. The card exists to be recognised
mid-scroll and then get out of the way — not to be admired, not to argue a
position, not to summarise what it cannot cite.

Space is held, not filled. The canvas is mostly the page's own paper —
`--background`, the same off-white the site itself sits on — because a share
card that looks like a different, louder product than the page it links to is
already lying a little. A single thin rule, the site's own muted gold
(`--accent-gold`), separates the identifying mark from the record it is
introducing; nothing else divides the space. Margins are generous and
symmetrical — the brief's 60px crop-safety floor is a minimum here, not a
target — because a masthead crowded to its own edges reads as anxious, and
this page is not.

Colour is inherited, not invented: `--primary`'s deep teal-navy for the mark
and the name, `--text-secondary` for the one supporting line, `--border` for
the rule if the gold reads too warm against a given crop. No gradient, no
photographic texture, no party colour standing in for identity — the palette
is exactly the tokens the site already spends its credibility on, because a
card that improvises its own colour story the moment it leaves the page is
the first small crack in "this is the same publication." Every one of these
choices should look obvious in hindsight and be the product of measuring, not
guessing — a masthead this plain is far easier to get slightly wrong (a rule
one weight too heavy, a margin one step too tight) than to get right, and
getting it right is the entire craft on offer here.

Typography carries the actual information and is treated with more care for
that reason, not less: the site's own Source Serif 4 sets the name or the
site title, at a size chosen so it survives the crop down to roughly half the
canvas without losing its footing, and IBM Plex Sans — the same face the
interface's own labels use — sets the one supporting line beneath it in a
smaller, quieter weight. No display face, no condensed stunt cut, no more
than two weights on the whole canvas. The lion mark sits to one side at a
modest, legible size — present as a signature, not blown up as a hero image
— and is never recoloured, redrawn, or stylised past what the site's own
files already are.

What the card must never do is assert a fact the page beside it cannot carry.
No score, no date, no evidence status, no figure — a share card is not a
citation, and the moment it starts to look like one is the moment it goes
stale the first time the record underneath it is corrected. The only text
permitted is a name or the site's own title, and a plain, unevaluative role
label taken verbatim from the record. Everything else this system might have
reached for — a photograph, a texture, a gradient with a mood — is left out
on purpose, and the restraint of leaving it out is the actual design
decision here, made with the same deliberateness a louder canvas would have
spent on adding more.
